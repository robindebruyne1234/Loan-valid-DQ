# Build review 1: client-grain privacy and the worked example

Written immediately after the build, against the repository and the database
rather than from memory. Where a claim could not be verified it says so.

---

## 1. What was asked, and what was built

The request was for a worked example that is cleaned, and private at client
grain so that *"other clients cannot see each other's secret data"*. The brief
words Task 3 one level up from that -- "each user sees only rows for their own
clients", which describes a relationship manager with a book -- so the build
covers both grains rather than choosing between them.

| | |
|---|---|
| Principals | **6**: 3 staff books, 1 deliberately unassigned, 2 self-service customers |
| Policies | **7**, unchanged by the new grain |
| Isolation attempts | **13** targeted reads by one customer of another, **0** succeeded |
| Tests | **156** passing (up from 145), of which **27** are entitlement tests |
| Pipeline | `make clean all` from empty: 12 of 12 steps, 10 of 10 LLM answers matching |
| New deliverables | `docs/WORKED_EXAMPLE.md` (generated by `make demo`), 7 deck slides |

### The design, in one paragraph

`sec.app_user` gained one nullable column, `subject_client_id`, and a check
constraint making it mutually exclusive with portfolio-based entitlement.
`sec.v_my_clients` gained a `UNION` that resolves it. That is the whole change.
A relationship manager is a principal that *holds* a book; a customer is a
principal that *is* a client. Both are answered by the same view, so the seven
row level security policies never learned that customer logins exist -- they
ask "which clients may the caller see" and get a one-row answer instead of a
1,481-row one.

The demonstration pair was chosen to make the test hard rather than easy:
clients 11409 and 3763 both live in north Bohemia, so **both sit inside
`rm_north`'s book**. The manager sees both; neither sees the other. Isolating
two customers in different portfolios would have proved nothing that a
portfolio boundary did not already prove.

## 2. What was verified, and how

Four things, in descending order of how much they are worth.

### 2.1 The isolation holds, and the test can detect that it does not

Measured by authenticating as each principal and counting, never by reading
the policy. The matrix is regenerated on every `make demo`:

| Principal | Clients | Accounts | Loan value | Sees 11409 | Sees 3763 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `client_11409` | 1 | 1 | 39,576 | yes | **no** |
| `client_3763` | 1 | 1 | 123,216 | **no** | yes |
| `rm_north` | 1,481 | 1,241 | 25,392,348 | yes | yes |
| `rm_south` | 1,386 | 1,157 | 28,938,528 | no | no |
| `rm_unassigned` | 0 | 0 | 0 | no | no |
| `cro_global` | 5,369 | 4,500 | 103,261,740 | yes | yes |

A passing negative test proves nothing unless it can fail, so the isolation
was deliberately broken -- `client_11409` was converted to a staff user holding
`NORTH_BOOK` -- and the suite was re-run. Three tests failed and the role saw
1,481 clients instead of 1. The protection was then restored and the suite
re-run clean. **The tests detect a real leak**, which is the only reason the
green run means anything.

### 2.2 Thirteen targeted attempts, all blocked

Row counts can pass while a targeted lookup leaks, so the attempts name the
other customer's own account id rather than counting rows. Two distinct
mechanisms do the blocking, and they are not equally strong:

- **Six reads through the published views, one straight to the base table**:
  the query runs and returns **0 rows**. This is a *policy*. It is the one that
  matters for an AI assistant, because it means a generated query never has to
  be inspected for whose data it asks about.
- **Reads of `stg`, `raw` and `sec.app_user`, plus editing the entitlement,
  disabling the policy, and `SET ROLE`**: refused outright. This is a
  *privilege*, and it is the weaker claim -- it stops the statement rather than
  narrowing the answer.

Separating the two in the write-up was deliberate. "Thirteen attacks blocked"
sounds stronger than it is if six of them were blocked by a `GRANT`.

### 2.3 The policy set did not change

The claim this project makes about its entitlement model is that the filter
lives in one place. Adding a second kind of principal would undermine it if it
had needed a policy of its own, so two tests pin it: the policy count still
equals the number of protected tables, and no policy definition mentions
`subject_client_id`. Verified: **7 policies, 7 protected tables, 0 mentions**.

### 2.4 Onboarding without code changes, performed rather than asserted

The brief asks how a new user is onboarded. `make demo` creates a third
customer live, shows them seeing nothing before the insert and exactly their
own record on the next statement, shows them isolated from the example client,
revokes with one `UPDATE`, and cleans up. The output in the document is that
run, not an illustration of it.

## 3. What this build got wrong

Two defects, both found during the work and both mine.

### 3.1 A wrong number in my own demonstration query

The query the worked example runs as each principal totals the loan book. I
wrote `sum(DISTINCT l.amount)` as a reflex guard against join fan-out. There is
no fan-out on that path -- one owner row per account, one loan per account --
and the DISTINCT silently discarded the 37 loans that share an amount with
another loan. Bank-wide it reported **98,780,760 CZK against a true
103,261,740**: a 4,480,920 CZK understatement.

This is worse than an ordinary bug because of where it was. A demonstration
built to prove that a governed product returns trustworthy numbers was itself
returning a wrong one, and it would have appeared in a document, on a slide,
and in front of a reviewer. It was caught by checking the total against the
base table rather than by reading the query.

Fixed, and pinned: `test_the_demo_question_totals_correctly_for_an_unrestricted_caller`
compares the demo query's total against `sum(amount)` from `core.fact_loan`, so
it cannot regress quietly. The comment in the source says why the DISTINCT is
absent, so nobody tidies it back in.

### 3.2 An undocumented column, caught by the project's own gate

Adding `principal_type` to `ai.my_entitlement` broke `make semantic`:

```
ERROR | semantic layer -> ai.my_entitlement.principal_type: present in the
view but not described (an undocumented column is one the model will guess at)
```

The pipeline refused to build until the column was described. That is the gate
working exactly as designed, and it is worth recording as a success rather than
a stumble -- it is the difference between a semantic layer that is maintained
and one that is aspirational. The cost was two minutes.

## 4. What is weak

Honestly, and before anyone asks.

**The identity mapping is assumed correct and is unverifiable here (A35).**
The product enforces the `db_role -> client_id` mapping it is given. If that
mapping is wrong, the wrong person sees a complete, correct-looking view of
somebody else's finances, and nothing in this build could detect it. In a real
deployment that insert is the output of an identity-proofing process and the
weakest link in the whole chain is upstream of everything tested here.

**There is no read audit (A38).** The model controls who *can* read; it does
not record who *did*. For a bank that is a regulatory requirement, not a
refinement, and it is the largest missing control in this submission.

**Nothing is masked.** A customer sees their own birth date in full, and a
relationship manager sees it for all 1,481 of their clients. Field-level
masking is a separate control this build does not implement.

**Role-per-user still does not pool.** Correct for a demonstration and named in
the DDR; it becomes the first real engineering problem the moment a customer
portal with concurrent users is put in front of it. A self-service surface
makes this materially more pressing than it was when only a handful of staff
logins existed.

**Two kinds of row in one table.** `user_type` plus a nullable column is weaker
modelling than two tables sharing a supertype. The check constraint carries it
at this size. A third kind of principal -- a service account, an auditor --
should trigger the split rather than a third flavour.

**The customer/disponent question is unresolved (A36, D022).** A customer sees
accounts they own, not the 869 cases where they may operate an account they do
not own. That is the safer default and it is consistent with the staff rule,
but it is a business decision made here by an engineer, and a real deployment
should take it to someone who can say what the bank tells its customers.

**The demo pair is hard-coded.** Clients 11409 and 3763 are constants in
`src/config.py`. The isolation tests would not notice if the data changed
underneath them in a way that made the pair uninteresting -- for instance if
3763 moved out of `rm_north`'s region, `test_the_relationship_manager_sees_both_customers`
would fail loudly, which is the right behaviour, but the choice of a *good*
pair is not automated.

---

## 5. What I would do next, in order

1. **Add the read audit.** Largest missing control, and the one a regulator
   asks about first. A `log_statement` policy plus a table of who-read-what,
   or `pgaudit` if it can be installed.
2. **Decide the disponent question** with a business owner and implement
   whichever answer they give (D022). One predicate either way.
3. **Property-test the isolation** rather than testing one pair: for a random
   sample of client logins, assert that each sees exactly one client and that
   the union of what any two of them see is empty. That catches a class of bug
   a fixed pair cannot.
4. **Mask the birth date** in the customer-facing views, or decide explicitly
   that a customer may see their own in full and a manager may not see theirs.
5. **Then connection pooling**, because by that point the thing is worth
   putting real traffic through.

---

## 6. Would I ship it?

As a submission for this brief: yes, and the worked example is the part I would
put in front of a reviewer first, because it is the only artefact where every
claim in the whole build can be checked against one person in about three
minutes.

As a production customer portal: no, and not because the entitlement model is
wrong -- it is the part I am most confident in, because it is enforced by the
engine and tested by authenticating rather than by reading. It is missing the
audit log and the masking, and the identity mapping it depends on sits outside
it. Those are three named, scoped pieces of work, not a redesign, and the
reason they are nameable is that the privacy boundary is in one place.
