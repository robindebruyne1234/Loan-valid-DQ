# ASSUMPTIONS

Every interpretation that could reasonably have gone another way, with an ID.
The deliverables reference these IDs, so a reviewer can see exactly what was
assumed where. The same interpretations appear as comments next to each query
in `sql/60_reference_queries.sql` and as `interpretation` entries in
`dq/results/reference_results.json`.

Where an assumption is material, the figure that makes it material is given --
an assumption nobody can size is not reviewable.

## Question interpretation

| ID | Question | Assumption | Why | What changes if wrong |
|----|----------|------------|-----|-----------------------|
| **A1** | Q1 | "Region" is the region of the **client's own district** (`client.district_id -> district.A3`), not of the district their account is registered in. | The question says "clients ... per region", so the client is the subject and their own district is their region. | Material: 409 of 4,500 owners (9.1%) live in a different district than their account. Using the account district moves those clients between regions. Q5 deliberately uses the other reading (A8). |
| **A2** | Q1 | **All** clients are counted, including any who hold no account. | The question says "clients", not "clients with an account". | None in this copy: every one of the 5,369 clients has at least one disposition (rule `RI_CLIENT_WITHOUT_DISP` reports 0). The flagged count would be published if it were not 0. |
| **A3** | Q2 | "Granted per year" is dated by `loan.date`, the loan start date, and "total amount" is the sum of the **principal**. | It is the only date the loan table carries. Principal is what "amount of loans granted" means; `payments * duration` is the gross repayment, a different number. | Using `payments * duration` raises every yearly total by the interest, roughly 10-20%. |
| **A4** | Q3, Q10 | "Problematic" = loan status **B** (finished, not repaid) or **D** (running, client in debt). | The published status meanings: A and C are healthy, B and D are not. Materialised once as `core.fact_loan.is_problematic`. | Counting only B (realised losses) gives 31 of 682 = 4.55% instead of 76 of 682 = 11.14%. The definition is the answer, which is why it lives in the model and not in a query. |
| **A5** | Q4, Q8 | "The card of the account owner" is the card on the **owner's** disposition. A card held by a disponent on the same account is not attributed to the owner. | Both questions say "of the account owner" / "account owners holding". A disponent is not the owner. | 0 rows differ in this copy (rule `CON_CARD_HELD_BY_DISPONENT` reports 0), but the rule and the view enforce it so a future feed cannot change the answer silently. |
| **A6** | Q4 | Loans whose owner holds **no card** are reported as an explicit `no card` category, not dropped. | Dropping them would make Q4's loan counts disagree with Q2 and Q3 over the same 682 loans. | The `no card` group holds the large majority of loans; dropping it would change the reported averages into a statement about card holders only. |
| **A7** | Q5 | "Incoming" = transaction type `PRIJEM` (`direction = 'credit'`), summed on the **unsigned** amount. | The question asks for incoming volume, not a net flow. | Netting credits against debits would rank districts by net accumulation, a different business question. |
| **A8** | Q5 | The district is the district the **account** is registered in, not the owner's own district. | The money arrives at an account, not at a person. | Material for the same 9.1% as A1. Stated explicitly because Q1 and Q5 deliberately use different districts. |
| **A9** | Q6 | All twelve months of 1996 are reported, with zero where no account was opened, by driving the query from `core.dim_date`. | An omitted month reads as "no data", which is a different claim from "no accounts". | Every month of 1996 has accounts in this copy, so the zero-fill does not change a number here -- it changes whether the answer is trustworthy on a different copy. |
| **A10** | Q7 | Age is **exact**: date of birth to loan start in years including the fractional part. | "Average age at loan start" is a duration, and whole-year differences bias the average by about half a year. | A calendar-year difference (`year(loan) - year(birth)`) reports ages roughly 0.5 years higher. |
| **A11** | Q7 | The owner is the `OWNER` disposition of the loan's account; owners whose birth number does not decode are excluded and the covered population is reported. | A loan has no person of its own; it reaches one through the account owner. | 0 owners are excluded in this copy (all 5,369 birth numbers decode). 6 loans carry an owner recorded as under 15, so Q7 reports the average with and without them (rule `VAL_OWNER_AGE_AT_LOAN_START`, warn). |
| **A12** | Q8 | The population is account **owners** whose own disposition holds a gold card. | As A5. | See A5. |
| **A13** | Q8 | The unit counted is the **owner row** (one per account), with `count(distinct client)` reported beside it. | A client owning two accounts, each with a gold card, is two card-holder relationships but one person. | Both numbers are published, so neither reading is hidden: in this copy they are equal (53 male / 35 female, 88 distinct clients), so the ambiguity happens not to bite. |
| **A14** | Q9 | The balance at 1998-12-31 is the balance after the **last posting on or before** that date, from `core.fact_account_month_end_balance`, with ties on a date broken by the **highest `trans_id`**. | `trans_id` is not chronological within a day, so a tie-break is unavoidable; it is documented and applied identically in the reference SQL, in the model and in the semantic layer. | 183 accounts have several postings on their latest date (rule `CON_INTRADAY_ORDER_AMBIGUOUS`, warn), so for those the closing balance is a documented choice rather than a fact. |
| **A15** | Q9 | The average is over accounts with a **known** balance on that date; an account with no posting is excluded, not counted as zero. | A zero would be a claim about the account's balance that the data does not support, and it would bias the average downwards. | 0 accounts are excluded in this copy (all 4,500 have postings), so the answer covers the whole book. |
| **A16** | Q10 | The share is problematic loans divided by **all loans of accounts with that statement frequency** (a within-frequency rate), not each frequency's share of all problematic loans. | "Which frequency has the highest share of problematic loans" is a rate per frequency. | The other reading ranks by volume and would favour the monthly frequency, which carries most accounts. The full ranking with loan counts is returned so a small denominator is visible. |

## Pipeline and environment

A17-A20 are cleaning assumptions and are cross-referenced from the cleaning
ledger; they were written before that section existed and are left in place
rather than renumbered.

| ID | Area | Assumption | Why | What changes if wrong |
|----|------|------------|-----|-----------------------|
| **A17** | Dates | A two-digit source year is always `19xx`. | The dataset runs 1993-1998 and was published in 1999; there is no 20xx data. | A `20xx` reading would move dates out of the validity window, which rule `VAL_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE` would block -- so the assumption fails loudly rather than silently. |
| **A18** | Gender | `birth_number` encodes sex as month + 50 for women, and that is the only available source for gender. | Documented with the dataset; `CON_GENDER_MATCHES_BIRTH_NUMBER` checks the decode against the raw digits on every run. | Q8 is entirely built on it. The rule turns a decoder regression into a build failure. |
| **A19** | Currency | All amounts are in the same currency (CZK) and no conversion is needed. | Single-country dataset from one bank; no currency column exists. | If a second currency appeared, every sum (Q2, Q4, Q5, Q9) would be adding unlike units. There is no rule that could detect it from this schema, which is why it is written down. |
| **A20** | Rounding | Source amounts carry one decimal place, and the per-account balance chain is therefore reconciled with a tolerance (1 CZK rounding band, 100 CZK materiality band) rather than exactly. | Measured: every one of 1,056,320 amounts has a zero in the second decimal. | 631 of 4,500 accounts reconcile exactly, 4,364 within 1 CZK, 55 are off by more than 100 CZK. Without the bands the rule would report 3,869 "defects" and hide the 55 that matter. |
| **A21** | Entitlements | A user entitled to a client sees that client, the accounts that client **owns**, and everything hanging off those accounts. A disponent relationship does not grant the portfolio holder access to the account. | "Each user sees only rows for their own clients": ownership is the relationship a relationship manager is accountable for. | Including disponent relationships would widen every portfolio; `sec.portfolio_client` plus the policy predicate is where that choice is changed, and it is one SQL predicate, not a model change. |
| **A22** | Entitlements | Reference data (`dim_district`, `dim_date`, `ref_codes`) is **not** entitlement filtered. | They contain no client information, and filtering them would break every group-by for every user. | If districts were considered sensitive, the policy would be added to those tables too; nothing else in the design changes. |
| **A23** | Data copy | The public Berka copy is the primary dataset, because `data/raw/provided/` is empty. Its row counts match the published reference exactly, so it carries no injected defects. | No bank-supplied copy was received (decision point HD1). | On the bank's copy the counts would differ, `REC_ROWCOUNT_RAW_TO_STG` and `REC_LOAN_STATUS_DISTRIBUTION` would fire, and the same 123 rules would run unchanged against `data/raw/provided/`. |
| **A24** | LLM | The LLM is treated as an untrusted query author: it sees the semantic layer and the question, never the database, and its SQL is parsed and rewritten before execution. | It is the only assumption under which "an LLM-generated query cannot bypass the filter" can be true. | If the model were trusted, the entitlement filter would have to live in the prompt -- which is a request, not a control. |

## Cleaning and extraction

Added in the cleaning revision. These are the assumptions the transformation
steps rest on -- what the pipeline takes the source to mean when it types,
normalises, decodes and derives. Each names the ledger step
(`docs/CLEANING_LEDGER.md`) it governs, so an assumption can be traced to the
code that acts on it. A17-A20 above belong to this group too.

| ID | Step | Assumption | Why | What changes if wrong |
|----|------|------------|-----|-----------------------|
| **A25** | C03 | The placeholder list (`''`, `'?'`, `'NA'`, `'N/A'`, `'NULL'`, `'-'`, `'unknown'`) is the complete set of ways this source writes "missing". | Derived by profiling every text column, not assumed up front; the profiler reports the full distinct-value distribution per column and no other placeholder-shaped value appeared. | An eighth spelling would be counted as a real value, overstating completeness for that column. It would not corrupt anything downstream, and the profile report is where it would show up. Low risk, cheap to extend: one tuple in `src/config.NULL_PLACEHOLDERS`. |
| **A26** | C03, C09 | A NULL after cleaning means "the source did not record this", never "the pipeline lost it". | Every value the cleaning dropped or changed is kept in a `<column>_raw` column and listed in `_conversion_notes`, so the two cases are distinguishable by construction. | This is the assumption the whole ledger exists to make checkable. If it were false, every completeness number would be measuring the loader rather than the source. |
| **A27** | C09 | `operation_code IS NULL` on a transaction means a bank-generated posting, not a missing value. | Measured: all **183,114** rows with no operation carry `k_symbol = UROK` (interest credited), and no other category has a NULL operation. The bank books interest without a customer-facing operation. | If some of those NULLs were genuinely missing data, the completeness rule on that column would have to be `warn` rather than `info`, and "transactions by operation" would be understating one category. The measurement is re-run every build, so a mixed population would show up as UROK no longer being the only value. |
| **A28** | C09, D017 | The code sets are **open**: the source may contain a code the catalogue does not know, and the right response is to fail the build rather than guess a label. | It already happened. `LEASING` appeared on 341 standing orders and was not in the map; because the catalogue check did not cover that table, nothing failed and the rows shipped unlabelled. | The assumption now has teeth: `check_codes_against_staging` covers every coded column and `tests/test_dq.py::test_every_present_code_resolves_to_a_label` fails if any staged row carries a code with no label. A new code stops a build, which is the intended cost. |
| **A29** | C10 | `PRIJEM` is the only credit type; `VYDAJ` and `VYBER` are both debits. | Documented with the dataset, and consistent with the reported running balance: the balance chain reconciles for 4,364 of 4,500 accounts under this sign convention and would reconcile for almost none under any other. | Every monetary answer that nets or directs money (Q5, Q9) depends on it. The balance reconciliation rule is the check: a wrong sign convention would make it fail at scale rather than for 55 accounts. |
| **A30** | C10, C20 | `balance_after` as reported by the bank is authoritative; the pipeline never recomputes it from the amounts. | It is the bank's own record of the account state after the posting. Recomputing would substitute this pipeline's arithmetic for the bank's ledger. | The 55 accounts whose chain does not reconcile beyond 100 CZK are reported as a defect rather than corrected (D013). If the bank's balances were known to be unreliable, the derived balance would become the published one and those 55 would become silent. |
| **A31** | C19, C20 | The extract is a point-in-time snapshot with no history: no attribute of a client, account or district changes over time within it. | There is no effective-date column anywhere in the eight files, and no table carries two versions of the same key. | No slowly changing dimensions are needed, which is why `dim_client` and `dim_district` are plain. A feed with history would need SCD2 on at least district attributes, since the 1995/1996 socio-economic columns are already two snapshots flattened into one row. |
| **A32** | C19 | The dataset window is 1993-01-01 to 1998-12-31, and `dim_date` is generated to exactly that span. | Measured from the data: the earliest and latest transaction dates are exactly those two days. | A date outside the window would not join to `dim_date` and would drop out of any date-driven aggregate. `VAL_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE` is the rule that makes that fail loudly rather than quietly. |
| **A33** | C16 | Row order within a source file carries no meaning; the only ordering is by the date and key columns. | The files are flat extracts with no sequence column beyond the surrogate ids, and `trans_id` is demonstrably not chronological within a day (`CON_INTRADAY_ORDER_AMBIGUOUS`, 183 accounts). | If file order were the true posting order, the Q9 tie-break would have a better answer available and those 183 accounts would stop being ambiguous. `_source_row` is retained on every row, so that reading could be tested without a reload. |

## Privacy and identity

Added with the client-grain work. A21 and A22 above belong to this group too.

| ID | Area | Assumption | Why | What changes if wrong |
|----|------|------------|-----|-----------------------|
| **A34** | Identity | A self-service customer authenticates as their own database login role, and that role is never shared, pooled or re-pointed. | Same reasoning as A21/D009 for staff: `session_user` cannot be changed by any statement the caller or an LLM emits, because changing it needs a password neither ever sees. | This is the load-bearing assumption of the whole privacy claim. If customer logins were pooled behind one shared role, the entitlement would resolve to that shared role and every customer would see every other customer's data. Connection pooling is therefore named in the DDR as the first thing that must be redesigned, not quietly added. |
| **A35** | Identity | A customer is entitled to exactly the client record that `sec.app_user.subject_client_id` names, and the mapping from a real person to that client id is correct and is made outside this system. | This dataset has no identity documents, no logins and no names; the mapping from "the person at the counter" to `client_id` is the bank's KYC process, not a data-product concern. | The product enforces the mapping it is given. A wrong mapping shows the wrong person's data with the full force of a correct-looking entitlement, and nothing in this build could detect it. In a real deployment the insert into `sec.app_user` is the output of an identity-proofing process and should be auditable on that basis. |
| **A36** | Identity | A customer sees the accounts they **own**, not the accounts they may merely operate as a disponent. | Consistency with A21 and the safer default for a privacy boundary (D022). | 869 clients hold a disponent relationship. Under the other reading each of them would additionally see that account and its postings. It is one predicate in `sec.v_my_accounts` and it is a business decision, not a technical one. |
| **A37** | Privacy | Reference data (districts, calendar, code catalogue) is not sensitive, so a customer may read all 77 districts even though they belong to one. | Same as A22: filtering it would break every group-by, and a district list reveals nothing about any client. | If district-level data were considered identifying in combination with other attributes, the policy would extend to it. Nothing else in the design changes. |
| **A38** | Privacy | No read audit is required for this submission. | The brief asks who *can* see what, not what was actually read. | For a real bank this is a regulatory gap, not a simplification. It is listed in the worked example's limits and in the build review as the largest missing control. |

## Identity, access and authentication

Added with the two-factor and access-grant work. A34-A38 above belong here too,
and A36 is now superseded by D023.

| ID | Area | Assumption | Why | What changes if wrong |
|----|------|------------|-----|-----------------------|
| **A39** | Access | A customer may see every account they are authorised to OPERATE, including accounts they do not own. | What the bank tells its customers their app will show. Supersedes A36, which narrowed them to ownership; the decision was taken to the business reading and settled (D023). | 869 clients hold a disposition on an account they do not own. Under the narrow reading each of them sees one fewer account. It is one branch in `sec.v_access_grant`. |
| **A40** | Access | A disposition grants the ACCOUNT and nothing else -- not the owner's client record, not their other accounts. | Authorising someone to pay a bill from your account is not authorising them to read you. Nobody signing that arrangement would expect otherwise. | If a disposition granted the owning client instead, every disponent would see their co-holder's full customer record. This is the single most consequential line in the access model, and it is tested by name. |
| **A41** | Access | A staff portfolio grants `view` and never `operate`. | Being accountable for a client is not authority to move their money. | Nothing acts on `operate` in this read-only product, so the distinction costs nothing today and cannot be recovered later if it is dropped now. A test pins it. |
| **A42** | Identity | An opaque `client_ref` is sufficient protection for an identifier that appears in a URL or on a statement; it does not need to be rotated per session. | It is unguessable without the salt, and row level security stops access regardless. The reference prevents enumeration and correlation, not access. | If a reference leaked in bulk, rebuilding under a new salt reissues all of them. Per-session identifiers would be stronger and would make every support conversation impossible. |
| **A43** | Authentication | A time-based one-time code is an adequate second factor for this demonstration. | It is the standard most people already have an app for, and it is verifiable offline against RFC 6238. | TOTP is phishable in real time: a victim relaying a live code to an attacker defeats it. A deployment handling real money should prefer a phishing-resistant factor (WebAuthn/passkeys). The session model here would not change; only the factor would. |
| **A44** | Authentication | Storing TOTP shared secrets in a recoverable form is acceptable here. | Verification requires the secret, so it cannot be hashed. | `sec.auth_factor.secret` is the most sensitive column in the schema. A real deployment encrypts it with a key held outside the database, so that a dump of the data is not a dump of everyone's second factor. |
| **A45** | Authentication | Binding a session to `(db_role, pg_backend_pid)` is sufficient to stop replay. | A stolen session id is useless on a different connection, and the holder cannot forge `session_user` either. | Backend pids are reused after wraparound. A replay would need the same pid, the same role and the session still inside its 30-minute window; the combination is thin but not impossible, and a production system would add the backend start time to the binding. |
| **A46** | Demo | Printing the one-time code in the demo console does not weaken the claims being demonstrated. | The control being shown is what the second factor *stops*, not where the code comes from; every other part of the path is the production one. | A system that can display your own second factor has one factor. It is marked as a demo affordance at every call site and is the first thing a deployment removes. |
