## Q1 — How many pupils does each wing have? {#k-q1}

*Real question: How many clients do we have per region?*

| Wing | Pupils |
|---|---:|
| south Moravia | 937 |
| north Moravia | 920 |
| central Bohemia | 664 |
| Prague | 663 |
| east Bohemia | 660 |
| north Bohemia | 561 |
| west Bohemia | 515 |
| south Bohemia | 449 |

- **How we understood it:** the wing of each pupil's **own home topic**, counting **all 5,369**
  pupils — also the 869 who are only helpers on someone else's book. (A1, A2)
- **The trap:** counting record books instead of pupils (4,500), or using the topic the book is
  filed under. 409 owners have a different home topic from their book.
- **Watch out:** places 3–5 differ by only four pupils, so the way you count decides their order.

:::say
"A pupil is a person, and a person has one home topic, so I count all 5,369 by where they belong."
:::

## Q2 — How big were the study plans given out each year? {#k-q2}

*Real question: What is the total amount of loans granted per year?*

| Year | Plans | Total size (points) |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1993 | 20 | 2,619,276 |
| 1994 | 101 | 13,379,904 |
| 1995 | 90 | 13,344,372 |
| 1996 | 117 | 18,317,676 |
| 1997 | 196 | 30,731,364 |
| 1998 | 158 | 24,869,148 |

- **How we understood it:** dated by the day the plan **started** (the only date a plan has); the
  size is the plan's own size, not "size × months". (A3)
- **Watch out:** 1993 is only half a year — the first plan started on 5 July 1993. All years
  together: 103,261,740 over 682 plans.

## Q3 — What share of study plans went wrong? {#k-q3}

*Real question: What percentage of our loans are problematic?*

**76 of 682 = 11.14%** (B: finished, not passed — 31 · D: running, falling behind — 45).

- **How we understood it:** "went wrong" = B or D, out of **all** plans. Written once on every plan.
  This definition still needs the school's OK. (A4)
- **The trap:** only counting B gives 4.55%. Counting by *size* instead of number gives 15.09% —
  the plans that went wrong were bigger ones.

## Q9 — The average point total at the end of December 1998 {#k-q9}

*Real question: What was the average account balance at the end of December 1998?*

**43,808.94 points** over all **4,500** record books — 76 of them had no entry in December, so their
November total was carried over.

- **How we understood it:** each book's total after its last entry on or before 31 December 1998;
  books with no total yet are left out, not counted as zero. (A14, A15)
- **Typical wrong answers:** forgetting the "who was last" rule creates double rows (43,508.31);
  only books with a December entry (44,209.53); averaging every December entry (46,908.00).
- **The puzzle:** same-day entries were decided by the highest number (chapter 7). With the domino
  rule (after the hand-in) it becomes 43,810.47.

:::say
"About 43,809 points over all 4,500 books, 76 carried over from November. I told the school the
'who was last' rule was a weak spot, and afterwards I fixed it: the average moves 1.53 points;
some single books move up to 1,200."
:::

## Q10 — Which report frequency has the most study plans that went wrong? {#k-q10}

*Real question: Which statement frequency has the highest share of problematic loans?*

| Report goes home … | Went wrong / plans | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| after every entry | 6 / 32 | **18.75%** |
| weekly | 11 / 91 | 12.09% |
| monthly | 59 / 559 | 10.55% |

- **How we understood it:** for each report frequency, the share of **its own** plans that went
  wrong. (A16)
- **The trap:** "which frequency has the most plans that went wrong" gives *monthly* (59) — but that
  is just because monthly has the most plans. That's the little robot's mistake.
- **Think about it:** 6 out of 32 is a small group. It is the highest share we saw, but it could be
  luck — "worth watching", not "proven".
